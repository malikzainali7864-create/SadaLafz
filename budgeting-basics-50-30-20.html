Budgeting basics: the 50/30/20 rule explained simply

By Hassan Tariq · September 8, 2026 · 5 min read

Most budgeting apps fail people for the same reason most diets fail people: too many categories, too much daily tracking, too much guilt when a week goes over. The 50/30/20 rule survives because it only asks you to sort money into three buckets, once a month.

The three buckets

Take your monthly income after tax and split it roughly like this:

  • 50% needs — rent, utilities, groceries, transport, minimum debt payments. The things that keep life running.
  • 30% wants — eating out, subscriptions, travel, hobbies. The things that make life enjoyable but aren't required.
  • 20% savings and debt payoff — anything beyond the minimum: an emergency fund, retirement contributions, extra payments on debt.

The numbers are a starting ratio, not a law. Someone in an expensive city with high rent might run closer to 60/20/20, and that's fine — the value of the rule is the habit of sorting spending into three intentions, not hitting an exact percentage.

Why three buckets works better than a detailed spreadsheet

A budget with forty line items asks you to make forty decisions a month. A three-bucket budget asks you to make three. Fewer decisions means the system survives a busy month, a stressful month, or a month where you simply don't feel like opening a spreadsheet — which, over a year, is most months for most people.

A budget you abandon in March isn't a stricter budget. It's a failed one.

Setting it up this week

  1. Write down your take-home pay for last month.
  2. Add up what you spent on true needs — be honest about what's a need versus a want.
  3. See where you land against 50/30/20, and adjust one bucket at a time rather than overhauling everything at once.
  4. Automate the 20% the day you're paid, so saving happens before spending has the chance to eat it.

The goal isn't a perfect ratio on a spreadsheet. It's a system simple enough that you're still using it in December.
