A first-timer's guide to Hunza Valley

By Sara Malik · September 3, 2026 · 7 min read

Hunza shows up in every "most beautiful valleys in the world" list for good reason, but most guides either overwhelm first-timers with a two-week itinerary or underwhelm them with a rushed weekend plan. Here's a middle path: four days, built around the things actually worth the long drive up.

When to go

Late September to mid-October brings the valley's famous autumn colours — golden poplars against snow-capped peaks — and is generally considered the best window. Spring, around April, brings apricot and cherry blossoms and fewer crowds. Peak summer is green and pleasant but busier and hazier for photography.

Getting there

Most travellers fly into Gilgit and drive roughly two and a half hours to Karimabad, Hunza's main town, along the Karakoram Highway. The overland route from Islamabad is spectacular but long, typically 18 to 20 hours by road, so most first-timers split it into two days or fly one way.

Where to stay

Karimabad has the widest range of guesthouses, most with balconies facing Rakaposhi peak — worth paying slightly more for. Altit and Ganish villages are quieter, walkable alternatives a short drive away if you'd rather avoid the main tourist strip.

The four viewpoints worth the drive

  • Eagle's Nest, Duikar — the classic sunrise viewpoint overlooking the entire valley and three peaks at once. Go for sunrise, not sunset; the light hits the peaks better.
  • Attabad Lake — a turquoise lake formed by a 2010 landslide, now a boating and photography stop about an hour further up the highway.
  • Baltit Fort — a restored 700-year-old fort in Karimabad itself; the terrace view alone justifies the ticket.
  • Passu Cones — jagged peaks near Passu village, best photographed from the suspension bridge in the early morning.
The valley rewards travellers who build in slow mornings rather than trying to see everything in one loop.

A simple four-day shape

  1. Day 1: Arrive Karimabad, settle in, sunset walk to Baltit Fort.
  2. Day 2: Sunrise at Eagle's Nest, afternoon at Altit Fort and village.
  3. Day 3: Day trip to Attabad Lake and Passu Cones.
  4. Day 4: Slow morning, local market, depart.

Four days is enough to leave with a real feel for the valley rather than a checklist of photos — and enough reason to plan the two-week version for next time.
