Slow mornings: building a routine that actually sticks

By Amina Raza · August 27, 2026 · 5 min read

Search "morning routine" and you'll find the same list everywhere: wake at 5am, cold shower, journal, meditate, exercise, all before most people's alarms go off. It works for the person who posted it. For most people trying to copy it wholesale, it lasts about nine days.

The real problem isn't discipline

It's that the routine was designed for someone else's energy, schedule and job. A routine built around a 5am wake time assumes a 9pm bedtime that a parent of a toddler, a night-shift nurse, or someone with a long commute simply doesn't have. When the routine doesn't fit the life, people don't fail at the routine — the routine fails them.

Design around your actual constraints

A better starting question isn't "what does a great morning routine look like" but "what time do I reliably wake up, and what can genuinely fit in the 20 minutes after that." For most people that's closer to two or three small things, not seven.

A routine you skip half the week isn't a routine. It's a wish.

A minimum version worth trying

  • Light before phone. Open a curtain or step outside for two minutes before checking notifications. This alone does more for alertness than most morning supplements.
  • One thing for your body. A short stretch, a walk to get coffee, ten squats — the specific movement matters less than doing something before sitting down for the day.
  • One thing for your mind. A page of a book, three lines in a notebook, or five minutes of quiet before the day's messages start arriving.

Three small anchors, done consistently, beat a seven-step routine done for a week and abandoned. The goal isn't an impressive morning — it's a repeatable one.
