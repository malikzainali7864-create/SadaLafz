Smartphone battery myths you should stop believing

By Bilal Khan · September 5, 2026 · 5 min read

Phone batteries are lithium-ion, and lithium-ion chemistry hasn't changed much in a decade — but the advice people repeat about it keeps mutating. Some of it was true for early smartphones and never got updated. Some of it was never true at all. Here's what actually matters.

Myth: you should let your battery drain to 0% sometimes

This was reasonable advice for older nickel-based batteries with "memory effect." Lithium-ion batteries have no such effect, and repeatedly draining to zero is actually one of the more stressful things you can do to one. Batteries are happiest cycling in a middle range, roughly 20% to 80%.

Myth: overnight charging ruins your battery

Modern phones stop charging once they hit 100% and only trickle small amounts of power to offset natural drain, so overnight charging on its own does very little damage. What causes more wear is keeping a phone at 100% and hot for long stretches — heat, not the act of charging, is the real stressor on battery chemistry.

Myth: closing background apps saves battery

On modern iOS and Android, apps you haven't touched in a while are already frozen in the background and use almost no power. Force-closing them can actually cost more battery, since reopening an app from scratch takes more energy than resuming a frozen one.

Heat is the real enemy of a phone battery — more than any charging habit.

What actually helps

  • Avoid leaving your phone in direct sun or a hot car — heat degrades battery capacity faster than any charging pattern.
  • Use the manufacturer's optimized or scheduled charging feature if your phone has one; it slows charging near 100% to reduce stress.
  • A case that traps heat during charging can quietly do more damage than charging overnight ever would.
  • Battery health naturally declines with age and cycles — a battery at 85% health after two years of daily use is normal, not a sign you did something wrong.

Most of the folklore around battery care solved problems from a decade ago. The one habit worth actually keeping is simple: keep your phone cool.
